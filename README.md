# Jequan Davis

**Founder and Project Lead, Written In The Genome** · Charlotte, NC

I build tools and archives for the stories hidden in our DNA. My work sits where genealogy, genetics, and software meet: primary records first, claims only as strong as the evidence under them, and privacy treated as a design rule rather than a setting.

## What I am building

**[Genotype Scout](https://scout.writteninthegenome.blog)**
A third-party analysis tool for raw DNA files people already have from a testing company. Upload a file and Scout reads it entirely on your own device: ancestry composition, haplogroups, chromosome painting, health marker education, and relationship tools built for endogamous families. Nothing is transmitted, nothing is stored on a server. Live and in active development, with a beta community of 129 testers.

**The Family Archive**
A public archive of Delmarva Afro-Indigenous family history: 354 primary documents, more than 4,800 citations, and 3,789 ancestor profiles covering the Nanticoke, Lenape, and Free People of Color families of Delaware, Maryland, Virginia, and New Jersey. Built to the Genealogical Proof Standard, with conflated records flagged rather than quietly merged. I compiled and confirmed its contents from three distinct sources, credited separately throughout: the Mitsawokett research collection, the research of Lynn C. Jackson, and my own genealogical research in the primary records.

**[Written In The Genome](https://writteninthegenome.blog)**
The blog and home base: African American genealogy, DNA ancestry, and family history, including the Tidewater Creole families of Delmarva.

**Companion tools**
DNA SuperKit Builder (merge and repair raw kits across companies and builds), Chromosome Phaser Studio (phasing and chromosome painting), and Haplotype Scout (uniparental and segment analysis). All run client-side, all under the same rule: a label must not outrun the measured data.

## How I work

I direct development rather than hand-writing code: I scope the problem, write the specification, set the acceptance gates, and verify every change against the real data and the live deployment before it ships. More than 150 pull requests opened in Genotype Scout alone, each one checked against its brief before merge. The standard is simple. If I cannot show the receipt, it is not done.

My background is operations: retail management running remodels with teams of 50-plus, automotive sales, and licensed insurance work across all 50 states. I am now building toward project management roles, where that operations history and this product work meet.
